MC-GS across acceleration factors: T1 + FLAIR, retrospective vd-Poisson undersampling.
a, b, Head-masked whole-volume PSNR, SSIM and NMSE of the 11 methods at acceleration factors 12, 16, 20 and 24 for T1 (a) and FLAIR (b). Colour: value on one scale per metric block (darker = better; NMSE in per cent, its scale inverted); zero filling (ZF) is below the scale and shown lightest. Bold with an outline = best of the 11 methods in that column. One vd-Poisson mask per acceleration factor for both contrasts.
c, Per-slice gain of MC-GS over GS-single and over the strongest non-GS method (CS-SENSE on T1, Joint SENSE on FLAIR): PSNR gain, SSIM gain and relative NMSE reduction. n = 597 slices per contrast and acceleration factor (transverse, coronal and sagittal slices whose head covers >= 5% of the slice; slices of one volume, not independent subjects). Above the dashed line = MC-GS better. Dot, median; bar, interquartile range; violin trimmed to the 0.5-99.5 percentiles. MC-GS is better in 85-100% of the slices, depending on the pair (source data); one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test, P < 1e-60 in every pair, not corrected for multiple comparisons.
One scorer and one ground truth for all methods; single subject, seed 42; GS-single = the registered arm (one scan fed to both channels of the joint model); ZS-SSDU with 5 residual blocks.
